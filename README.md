# Pip and the Missing Endings

Full description

Pip and the Missing Endings is a story-driven interactive adventure you play in your browser. It’s made for ages 8 to 12 and for anyone who likes gentle fairy tale adventures with real choices and plenty of jokes.

It starts on a rainy afternoon. You find a glowing golden book in the attic, and a tiny bookworm named Pip, who wears a monocle and snacks on boring words, pops out of its pages. A living ink Blot has been smudging the last page of every fairy tale, and without endings nobody gets their happily ever after. Pip needs a reader brave, kind, or clever enough to step into the book and fix them.

Book One: The Missing Endings takes you through four story worlds, which you can visit in any order, and then to a finale in the Tower of Unfinished Stories:

The Merry Greenwood. An archery contest with Robin Hood, Maid Marian, Little John and Friar Tuck, and a grumpy Sheriff who might just need a seat at the feast.
The Yellow Road. Rescue a Scarecrow, oil a Tin Woodman, and coach a Lion whose roar comes out as a squeak. Then meet a Wizard who is not quite what he seems.
The Enchanted Castle. A gruff, bookish Beast, a goose butler who honks eleven times, a cat chef, a squeaky suit of armor, and a rose that needs saving.
The Darkwood. Ticklish trees, a wolf who writes poetry, a girl in a red hood, and a gingerbread house full of friends. You light the lanterns that bring the stars back.
The Tower of Unfinished Stories. Climb with every friend you made, and give the lonely Blot an ending of his own.

Book Two: The Mixed-Up Pages picks up the next morning. Pip, who has trouble with hiccups, ate the Table of Contents, and now every hiccup sends a piece of it into the wrong story. Characters you know wander into new roles, and you set things right in four more worlds before the finale:

The Beanstalk Kingdom. A lonely giant who only wants someone to share his cupcakes, and a very sad goose.
The Midnight Ball. A clock stuck at 11:59, a stubborn pumpkin, and a very tired Cinderella.
The Three Pigs’ Village. A wolf with stage fright, a poetry hall to build, and nobody gets blown away.
The Name Game. A tiny spinner who has forgotten his own name, three golden clues, and a very long word to guess.
The Middle of the Book. Pip faces his hiccups, and you write the first line of the last chapter.
How it plays

The game is easy to learn:

You have 5 hearts and three powers: Courage, Kindness and Wits. You pick your hero style at the start.
Some choices ask for a power. The button shows what it needs and what you have, for example “Courage 3 · you have 3 ✓”.
If a check is too hard, nothing bad happens. You lose a heart and the story keeps going. If all your hearts run out, you take a funny nap and wake up with hearts again.
Collect coins and spend them in the little shops for power boosts.
Gather a Story Star from each world to open the finale.
Choices are usually two or three, and the story decides how it ends.
Built for easy reading
One or two short sentences per screen, so the story moves quickly and never feels crowded.
Tap anywhere on the story card to continue. A Back button lets you re-read.
Four text sizes, from Small to Extra large, with a clear, easy-to-read typeface.
Light and dark colors that follow your device, or that you can set yourself.
Optional Read to me uses your device’s voice.
Big, easy-to-hit buttons designed for phones and tablets.
Your place saves automatically after every screen, with a Continue button when you return.

Trophies

There are 52 trophies, and every one has a silly description, like Pie in the Sky, Squeaky Roar, Two Left Paws, Gourd Almighty and Reginald Who?. They pop up over the picture for several seconds, long enough to read, then slide away without covering the story or the buttons. A trophy room in the menu tracks everything you’ve found.
